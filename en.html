About · Zagreb, since 2014

Croatia's first crypto exchange, now the first with a MiCA licence

Electrocoin d.o.o. was founded in Zagreb on 3 February 20147 as one of the first fintech start-ups in Croatia focused on cryptocurrencies9. From a hobby project funded with €2,500 of savings10, it has grown into the country's largest crypto company12 and the first crypto-asset service provider licensed by HANFA under the EU's MiCA Regulation1.

5. Regulation: MiCA licence from HANFA

First entry in the register of crypto-asset service providers

Until 2026, Electrocoin operated as a registered virtual asset service provider (VASP) under the anti-money laundering act. On 9 April 2026, HANFA's Management Board authorised it to operate under Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, the first company in Croatia to receive such authorisation12. Other VASPs must obtain a licence by 1 July 20261; of the 16 registered companies, seven remain on the market12.

2. Acquisitions and partnerships: who and why

Your proposal, worked out candidate by candidate
Key point for strategy: a licence does not transfer when a company is bought. Buying an unlicensed company gives Electrocoin nothing on the regulatory side, because it already holds a licence. What has value is the customers (with their consent, because of GDPR), merchant contracts, technology, brand and people. That is why, in practice, acquiring assets and migrating customers is smarter than buying an entire company along with its liabilities.Own analysis based on the MiCA licensing rules.139
High potentialStrategic partnership

Adria Digital Exchange

A company founded in 2023 by the Zagreb Stock Exchange (24%) together with the technology firm Digital Assembly to develop virtual asset trading35. It was registered for custody, a trading platform and orders, but has no MiCA licence2728.

Opportunity: Electrocoin as the licensed executor and custodian for exchange members, brokers and banks. The exchange brings institutional clients; Electrocoin brings the licence and infrastructure. A stake in a joint venture, or a buyout of Digital Assembly's share.

High potentialCustomer migration

Fima Plus customers (fimacrypto.com)

On 29 July 2026, HANFA ordered Varaždin-based Fima Plus d.o.o. to stop providing crypto services immediately and return assets to its customers, because it had been operating without MiCA authorisation33.

Opportunity: an agreement for an orderly migration of customers to Electrocoin, with their consent. Customers keep their assets, and the regulator gets an orderly exit. Fast and cheap.

High potentialUser acquisition

Former Kriptomat and Binance users

Kriptomat (kriptomat.io) withdrew its MiCA licence application and shut down on 30 June 2026. It cites "hundreds of thousands of users" and is directing them to Kraken3637. After the deadline, Binance holds no licence in any EU country39.

Opportunity: a targeted "switch to the first Croatian licensed platform" campaign with Croatian-language support and free asset transfers. Advantages over Kraken: local language, PayCek and Croatian Post.

Medium potentialTo be checked

HODL Store, Blok Be, Fintrax

The three companies with the broadest range of services among the unlicensed. HODL Store and Blok Be also had a trading platform, and Fintrax had custody and orders27. There is almost no public information about their business.

Next step: financial statements (FINA), customer numbers and merchant contracts, then talks with the owners about taking over customers or the team.

Medium potentialTo be checked

Cana Pay, FiatBit, DUPLICO, Marinix275, BD Innovation Hub, Credos, M obrada

Smaller registered providers, mostly offering only exchange services (C, D) or orders (E, F, G)27. Judging by its name, Cana Pay targets payments, so it could be of interest for PayCek.

Next step: a quick check in the court register and with FINA; only those with active customers or merchants are of interest.

CautionRegulatory risk

Moneysail (Mercuryo)

Registered with the website mercuryo.io. In 2024, HANFA found that the company had no physical presence in Zagreb and ordered measures for its anti-money laundering system34.

1. Services Electrocoin may provide

Authorised by HANFA decision of 9 April 2026

KI1 · Custody and administration of crypto-assets on behalf of clients

held

The company holds clients' crypto-assets and controls access to them, instead of clients keeping their own private keys. It must have a custody agreement, a custody policy and segregated client assets, and it is liable for losses (Art. 75)64.

Example at Electrocoin: purchased bitcoin stays in the user's account on electrocoin.eu, with no wallet or keys of their own needed20.

KI3 · Exchange of crypto-assets for funds

held

Buying and selling crypto for euros. The company must have a non-discriminatory commercial policy and publish its price or the method for calculating it (Art. 77)64.

Example at Electrocoin: buying crypto by card or bank transfer, with cash at 55 Croatian Post offices, and euro payouts to an IBAN within 15–60 minutes2017. PayCek also runs on this service: the customer pays in crypto and the merchant receives euros14.

KI4 · Exchange of crypto-assets for other crypto-assets

held

Direct exchange of one cryptocurrency for another, without going through euros. The same rules on pricing and transaction disclosure apply as for KI3 (Art. 77)64.

Example at Electrocoin: swapping bitcoin for ether or USDC within a range of 60+ cryptocurrencies20.

2. Services Electrocoin may not yet provide

What each service means and examples of the activities it would enable

KI2 · Operation of a trading platform for crypto-assets

not held

An exchange where buyers and sellers are matched with each other through an order book. It requires operating rules, vetting of crypto-assets before admission to trading, resilient systems and public pre- and post-trade prices; dealing on own account is not allowed (Art. 76)64. It moves the company into capital Class 3 (€150,000)63.

Example activities: an order-book exchange like Kraken or Bitstamp; a regional exchange for tokens of domestic companies, as Adria Digital Exchange attempted35.

KI5 · Execution of orders for crypto-assets on behalf of clients

not held

The company executes a client's order on another trading venue, subject to a best execution obligation and an execution policy the client agrees to (Art. 78)64.

Example activities: limit and stop orders executed on external exchanges; brokering large (OTC) purchases across several exchanges to get the best price.

KI6 · Placing of crypto-assets

not held

Selling new tokens to buyers on behalf of the issuer. Before the agreement, the issuer must be informed of the placement method, fees, timing, price and target buyers, and conflicts of interest must be specifically managed (Art. 79)64.

Example activities: distributing a euro stablecoin or a domestic company's token to its own clients; tokenised bonds. In Croatia, Digital Assets (Bitcoin Store) holds it28.

KI7 · Reception and transmission of orders for crypto-assets

not held

The company receives an order from a client and passes it on to another party for execution. Orders must be transmitted promptly and accurately, without payments for order routing and without misuse of order information (Art. 80)64.

Example activities: a bank or exchange-office app that sends orders to a partner exchange; a corporate intermediary that collects orders from employees or clients.

KI8 · Providing advice on crypto-assets

not held

Personal recommendations to a client on what to buy or sell. It requires a suitability assessment (knowledge, objectives, financial situation), qualified staff, risk warnings and a suitability report (Art. 81)64.

Example activities: paid consultations for wealthier clients and companies entering crypto. General education, which Electrocoin already provides, is not advice because it is not a personal recommendation.

KI9 · Providing portfolio management on crypto-assets

not held

The company manages a client's crypto at its own discretion under an agreed mandate. The same suitability rules apply as for advice, plus quarterly reports to the client (Art. 81)64.

Example activities: ready-made portfolios (e.g. 70% BTC, 30% ETH) with automatic rebalancing; a crypto index for clients who do not want to pick for themselves.

KI10 · Providing transfer services for crypto-assets on behalf of clients

not held

Sending crypto from one address or account to another on behalf of a client. It requires a written agreement with the client covering procedures, security, fees and applicable law (Art. 82)64, along with the rule on tracing transfers (travel rule).

Example activities: sending crypto to an external wallet or to another user; paying merchants and employees in stablecoins through PayCek. Digital Assets and White Tech hold it28.

The badge colour is my assessment of how well each service would suit Electrocoin: green means a good fit with the existing business, yellow a moderate one, and orange a big step.

3. How to obtain a licence for the other services

An extension of the existing licence, not a new application from scratch
  1. Step 1

    Application for extension

    A licensed company requests the extension from HANFA, which granted its original licence, by supplementing and updating the information from its Art. 62 application. The request is processed under the same procedure as the first one (Art. 59(8))6061.

  2. Step 2

    Documentation for the new parts only

    Only what relates to the new service and what has changed is submitted, clearly marked. What HANFA already holds is not resent if the company confirms in writing that it remains accurate (HANFA Ordinance, OG 95/2025, Art. 5)65. Each service has its own section: the execution policy for KI5, the platform rules for KI2, staff expertise for KI8 and KI9, and a description of transfers for KI1061.

  3. Step 3

    Capital check

    Capital must exceed the higher of two amounts: the minimum for the class or one quarter of the previous year's fixed overheads (Art. 67)63. Electrocoin is already in Class 2, so KI5–KI10 do not change its class. Only KI2 requires Class 3 at €150,000, and the company's capital of €836,766 already covers that7.

  4. Step 4

    Submission to HANFA

    The application, on the prescribed form and with proof of the fee paid, is sent to HANFA6665.

  5. Step 5

    HANFA deadlines

    Acknowledgement of receipt within 5 working days, completeness check within 25 working days and a decision within 40 working days of a complete application. HANFA may request additional information up to the 20th working day, in which case the clock stops for up to 20 working days (Art. 63)62. Realistically, expect 3 to 5 months from submission.

  6. Step 6

    Registration and start of operations

    The new service is entered in HANFA's register and ESMA's register, and the existing MiCA business licence covers it in other EEA countries286.

I was unable to confirm the amount of HANFA's application fee; it is set by the Ordinance on the type and amount of fees (OG 157/2025)67. The 3 to 5 month estimate is my own.

4. What would make sense first

My assessment, not the company's plan
  1. KI10, crypto transfers. Closest to the existing business and a prerequisite for stablecoin payouts through PayCek. Two domestic competitors already hold it28.
  2. KI7 and KI5, orders. Needed for the banking module: the bank receives the order and Electrocoin executes it. The capital class stays the same.
  3. KI6, placing. Opens up partnerships with issuers of euro stablecoins and tokens. Digital Assets holds it28.
  4. KI8 and KI9, advice and portfolios. They require qualified staff and suitability assessments, but would turn the education the company already provides into revenue.
  5. KI2, exchange. The biggest step: new systems, admission rules and capital Class 3. It only makes sense if Electrocoin wants to become a regional trading venue.
